If you are applying to the University of Florida or already enrolled, you probably know that UF doesn't just take your high school GPA at face value. UF recalculates every applicant's GPA using its own formula, then uses that recalculated number for admission decisions. The same goes for the GPA you maintain as a current UF student — what your transcript shows and what your records office uses can be slightly different things.

Here is what you actually need to know to calculate your UF GPA correctly, plus what the UF admit GPA range looks like and how to maintain good academic standing.

The UF GPA Scale

UF uses the standard 4.0 grade-point scale with +/− grades. Here is how letter grades convert:

  • A = 4.0
  • A− = 3.67
  • B+ = 3.33
  • B = 3.0
  • B− = 2.67
  • C+ = 2.33
  • C = 2.0
  • C− = 1.67
  • D+ = 1.33
  • D = 1.0
  • D− = 0.67
  • E = 0.0

Notice UF uses E for failure, not F. The grade point value is the same — zero — but the letter is different from most state high schools. For admission purposes only, UF maps high school F to E during recalculation.

How UF Recalculates High School GPA for Admission

UF's admission GPA recalculation strips your transcript down to academic core courses: English, mathematics, science, social studies, and foreign language. Non-academic electives (PE, drama, study hall, etc.) are excluded from the recalculated number.

UF then applies weighting:

  • +1.0 bonus for AP, IB, and AICE courses
  • +0.5 bonus for Honors courses
  • No bonus for regular academic core courses

The result is a recalculated weighted GPA that typically runs higher than your school's reported number — but only if you have meaningful AP/IB/AICE coursework in core academic subjects. A student with all-A's in regular core classes will have a recalculated GPA close to 4.0. A student with all-A's in AP core classes can hit 5.0 recalculated.

UF Admit GPA Range

The mid-50% UF recalculated GPA for admitted freshmen is typically 4.4 to 4.6 weighted. The 25th percentile is around 4.4; the 75th percentile is around 4.6. Above 4.6 puts you in the strong-admit zone; below 4.4 makes admission less likely without compensating strengths (high test scores, strong essays, exceptional extracurriculars).

UF's admission process is holistic — the GPA is the heaviest single factor but not the only factor. Course rigor, SAT/ACT scores, essays, and extracurriculars all matter.

UF GPA for Current Students

Once you are enrolled at UF, GPA is calculated on the standard 4.0 scale using your UF coursework only. Your high school GPA is no longer relevant; UF is computing your college GPA from semester one onward.

Key academic-standing thresholds:

  • 2.0 cumulative — minimum to remain in good standing. Falling below triggers academic probation.
  • 3.5 semester — Dean's List threshold (varies slightly by college within UF).
  • 3.5 cumulative — typical threshold for Honors College admission and many graduate program prerequisites.
  • 3.7-3.8 cumulative — competitive for selective UF graduate programs (medical, law, MBA, PhD).

Common Mistakes Students Make Calculating UF GPA

  • Including non-academic electives in the recalculation estimate. PE doesn't count. Drama doesn't count. Strip them out before estimating your UF recalculated GPA.
  • Confusing E with F. A UF E is a 0.0, same as an F. Don't double-count or convert between them.
  • Forgetting that AP, IB, and AICE all get +1.0. Florida is one of the few states with widespread AICE coverage. AICE classes get the same +1.0 bonus as AP.
  • Mixing semester GPA and cumulative GPA. UF reports both. Most academic warnings, awards, and program prerequisites trigger on the cumulative number.
