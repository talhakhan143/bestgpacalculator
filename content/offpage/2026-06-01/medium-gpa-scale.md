# The GPA Scale, Demystified: 4.0 Unweighted, 5.0 Weighted, and What Colleges Actually Use

*Originally published at https://bestgpacalculator.online/gpa-scale*

If you have ever stared at your transcript and wondered why your "weighted" GPA is 4.3 while your "unweighted" GPA is 3.8, you are not the only one. The GPA scale is one of those things every student is supposed to understand and almost no one actually does — because schools, districts, and even individual colleges disagree on the math.

Here is the version nobody bothered to write down for you, in plain English.

## The 4.0 unweighted scale

The unweighted scale is the foundation everything else builds on. It is what most US high schools and almost every US college uses as the baseline:

- A = 4.0
- A− = 3.7
- B+ = 3.3
- B = 3.0
- B− = 2.7
- C+ = 2.3
- C = 2.0
- C− = 1.7
- D+ = 1.3
- D = 1.0
- D− = 0.7
- F = 0.0

The percentage equivalents — A = 93-96%, A− = 90-92%, B+ = 87-89%, and so on — are approximations. Different schools use slightly different cutoffs. A 92% might be an A− at one school and an A at another. Your school's grading chart is the authoritative source.

Notice there is no A+ in the unweighted scale at most US high schools. Some private schools and a handful of colleges award A+ as 4.3, but the vast majority cap at 4.0. The plus on an A+ exists for the transcript narrative, not the GPA arithmetic.

## The weighted scale, and why it exists

Weighted GPA exists to reward students who take harder courses. Without weighting, a student with five regular A's looks identical on paper to a student with five AP A's. Weighted GPA fixes that by adding bonus points for course rigor.

The standard US weighting (used by most public high schools):

- Honors classes: +0.5 to the unweighted grade points
- AP / IB / dual-enrollment: +1.0 to the unweighted grade points
- Regular classes: no bonus

So under standard weighting:

- An A in a regular class = 4.0
- An A in an Honors class = 4.5
- An A in an AP class = 5.0
- An A− in an AP class = 4.7

The weighted ceiling under standard weighting is 5.0. That is the absolute maximum if you take only AP classes and earn only A's. A weighted GPA above 5.0 means your school uses a non-standard scale (a few districts go up to 5.5 or even 6.0 — usually by treating Honors as worth +1.0 instead of +0.5).

## Scale reporting — what colleges see on your transcript

Every official transcript has a field called "scale" or "scale reporting." It tells admissions readers which scale produced the number on the transcript. Common values:

- **4.0 unweighted** — pure 4.0 maximum, no course-rigor bonus.
- **4.5 weighted** — Honors gets +0.5, AP/IB doesn't get a bonus.
- **5.0 weighted** — Honors +0.5, AP/IB +1.0 (most common in US public schools).
- **6.0 weighted** — Honors +1.0, AP/IB +2.0 (uncommon).
- **100-point scale** — Schools using 0-100 numeric grades. Colleges convert to 4.0.

The reason scale reporting matters: selective colleges use it to recalculate your GPA on their own internal scale. UC Berkeley, UCLA, and the rest of the University of California system all use a "UC capped weighted GPA." The University of Florida uses a recalculated GPA that strips out non-academic electives. These recalculations can move your reported GPA by 0.3 or more.

## How colleges recalculate

Most selective colleges do at least one of these:

- **Strip non-academic courses** — Cooking, PE, drama, study hall. They want to see how you did in core academic subjects only.
- **Convert to the college's own scale** — A school that uses 4.5 weighted gets converted to the college's 4.0 or its capped-weighted formula.
- **Apply a course-rigor lens separately** — Course rigor is reviewed alongside the GPA, not baked into the GPA itself.

The implication: the number on your transcript and the number a college actually uses to evaluate you are often different. That is also why "I have a 4.5 weighted GPA" is less informative than "I have a 4.5 weighted on the standard +0.5/+1.0 scale" — the second version tells the reader what to do with the number.

## Quick percentage-to-GPA approximation

If you need a rough conversion in your head: **percentage ≈ (GPA × 20) + 20**. So:

- 3.0 GPA → roughly 80%
- 3.5 GPA → roughly 90%
- 4.0 GPA → roughly 95-100%

This is intentionally a rough estimate. Real conversions depend on your school's grading bands. For an accurate conversion, run your numbers through a calculator that knows the standard cutoffs — try the [Percentage to GPA Calculator](https://bestgpacalculator.online/percentage-to-gpa-calculator) if you want the exact mapping.

## Other scales you may encounter

- **UK 1st / 2:1 / 2:2 / 3rd** — UK universities use degree classifications, not GPA. A First (70%+) is roughly a 4.0; a 2:1 (60-69%) is roughly 3.3-3.7.
- **German 1.0-5.0 (inverted)** — German universities use 1.0 as best, 5.0 as failing. The opposite of US GPA.
- **Indian CGPA on 10-point scale** — Common in Indian engineering universities. Conversion: CGPA × 0.4 ≈ US GPA.
- **Canadian 4.0** — Most Canadian universities use a 4.0 scale similar to the US, but with slightly different letter cutoffs.

## The bottom line

The GPA scale is not one thing — it is at least two (unweighted and weighted) and often more depending on your district. Your transcript should make clear which scale produced your number. If it doesn't, ask your registrar before applying anywhere. Colleges that recalculate will figure it out either way, but your application reads stronger when the math is transparent.
