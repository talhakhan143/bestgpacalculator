If you are applying to law school in 2026 or beyond, the GPA on your undergraduate transcript is not the GPA law schools actually see. The Law School Admission Council (LSAC) recalculates every applicant's undergraduate GPA from scratch on a standardized 4.33 scale through its Credential Assembly Service (CAS). The result — your CAS GPA — can differ substantially from what your school reported, and that is the number every US law school uses to evaluate you.

Here is what you need to know about how the LSAC GPA actually works, why it differs from your school GPA, and the realistic benchmarks for different tiers of law schools.

The LSAC 4.33 Scale

LSAC uses a 4.33 grade scale that recognizes A+ as 4.33. This is different from most US high schools and many undergraduate institutions, which cap A+ at 4.0.

  • A+ = 4.33 (only counted if your school awards A+ grades)
  • A = 4.00
  • A− = 3.67
  • B+ = 3.33
  • B = 3.00
  • B− = 2.67
  • C+ = 2.33
  • C = 2.00
  • C− = 1.67
  • D = 1.00
  • F = 0.00

How LSAC Differs From Your Undergrad GPA

Three differences matter most:

  • Retakes count both times. Most undergrad schools replace the original grade when you retake a course. LSAC averages both attempts into the CAS GPA. A C and a later A become 3.0 in your CAS — not 4.0.
  • A+ = 4.33. If your school awards A+ as 4.0, LSAC counts those grades as 4.33. This can move your CAS GPA slightly above your school GPA.
  • No AP credit included. AP courses you tested out of are excluded entirely. Only courses you took for a letter grade at an undergrad institution count.
  • Pass/fail and withdrawals excluded from GPA. They appear on your transcript but don't move your CAS GPA up or down.
  • Every transcript counts. Community college, study abroad, foreign — if you earned credit, it must be submitted and is averaged into your CAS GPA.

LSAC GPA Benchmarks by Law School Tier

Here is what the 25th-75th percentile CAS GPA looks like by school tier, along with typical LSAT ranges:

  • T14 (Yale, Harvard, Stanford, Columbia, NYU, etc.): 3.78 - 3.95 CAS GPA, 170-174 LSAT
  • Top 20 (Vanderbilt, USC, UCLA, Texas, etc.): 3.65 - 3.85 CAS GPA, 165-170 LSAT
  • Top 50: 3.4 - 3.7 CAS GPA, 158-165 LSAT
  • Top 100: 3.1 - 3.5 CAS GPA, 152-158 LSAT
  • All other ABA-accredited: 2.8 - 3.3 CAS GPA, 145-152 LSAT

A higher LSAT can compensate for a lower CAS GPA at some schools — the two numbers work together in admissions decisions.

The Retake Problem

This is the single biggest gotcha for law school applicants. If you got a C in Organic Chemistry as a sophomore and a year later retook it and got an A:

  • Your undergrad school: Probably replaces the C with the A. Your transcript GPA reflects only the A.
  • LSAC CAS GPA: Averages both grades. The C still counts.

This means students who relied on grade replacement to recover their undergrad GPA can be in for an unpleasant surprise when LSAC recalculates. The CAS GPA will be lower — sometimes by 0.1 or 0.2 or more, depending on how many retakes are involved.

Related Tools

Applying to other professional schools? The same "every grade counts including retakes" principle applies to medical school applications via AMCAS GPA and to physician assistant programs via CASPA GPA. Each professional admission service has slightly different rules but shares the principle: your school's reported GPA is not the GPA they use.
